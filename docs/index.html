Inside the drone

Our own 2.24 m VTOL drone for measuring how much water chaparral shrubs lose between dawn and midday, for planning prescribed burns. Click any part on the drone or in the list to see what it does, why we chose it, what it connects to and where to buy it.

Seven systems

Pick a part on the drone or below; it turns purple and lines show what it is wired or bolted to.

Power

Battery, power module and servo regulator: 648 Wh at 21.6 V, about 56 A in a hot hover.

Lift (hover)

Four motors, props and speed controllers that lift the drone off a hillside and land it.

Cruise (pusher)

The pusher that flies the drone on the wing over the plot.

Flight control

Flight controller, GPS, airspeed, radios and Remote ID.

Control surfaces

Four servos for the ailerons and the arch tail.

Airframe

Printed wing, fuselage and tail on carbon tubes. Takeoff mass 8.84 kg.

Mission sensors

What the mission needs: thermal camera, 1610 nm spot sensor, colour camera, air sensor and the computer that logs them. 356 g, 13 W.

What the drone carries for the mission

The parts above only make the drone fly. To see shrubs lose water between dawn and midday it needs to measure three things over the same plants on every flight: how warm the leaves are, how much short-wave infrared they reflect, and where every shrub is. These are the sensors for the first mission flights. Click a row to see it on the drone.

ItemWhyCost
1610 nm reference panel (white PTFE sheet, measured once on a lab spectrometer)Turns the spot sensor's readings into reflectance and catches drift between dawn and midday. Flown over before and after each flight.about $40, not priced
Warm and cool thermal targets: 2–3 insulated water tubs, each with a logging probeThe Lepton reads only to about ±5 °C; targets of known temperature in every flight correct it (Aragon et al. 2020 cut errors to about 1 °C this way).about $350, not priced
Weather meter on a tripod (Kestrel 5500 or an SHT45 logger)Air temperature, humidity and wind at the site through the day; the drone's air sensor is checked against it.$449 (Kestrel) or about $40

Not decided yet

  • A SWIR camera instead of the spot sensor: images of every shrub instead of one reading per 21 m spot. $2,250 a week to rent, $5,650 and up to buy; asked several companies.
  • A near-infrared reference band (about 860 nm, a cheap silicon photodiode as a third channel). Pairing a water band with a near-infrared band is the usual leaf-water index; filter not priced yet.
  • Centimetre GPS (RTK) to find the same shrub on every flight without ground targets. About $3,000; later.
