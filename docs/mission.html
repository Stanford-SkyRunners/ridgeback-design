Mission and range

We plan around an operating radius of about 1 km, set by the rule that the drone must stay in sight. So the motors and battery are sized for hover thrust and for time over the plants, not for distance.

Operating radius, range and endurance

TermOursWhat sets it
Operating radius: how far from the take-off pointabout 1 kmFAA rule 14 CFR 107.31: the pilot or an observer must see the drone at all times (text of the rule). The rule sets no distance; 1 km is how far we expect to see a 2.2 m drone reliably.
Range: distance on one charge71 kmLoiter speed × loiter time.
Endurance: time in the air59 minBattery energy against drag. This is the number that matters for the mission.

A field day

Water loss shows up as the difference between dawn and midday. We target three 15-minute surveys of the same 2 ha chaparral plot on one charge, within 1 km of the take-off point. The current estimate is 2.7 flights per charge.

Why dawn and midday, and why 15 minutes

  • Humphrey and Frankenberg, Biogeosciences 20:1789 (2023). Measured canopy water continuously under an oak: about 28 % of the pre-dawn water was gone by midday. A single daily measurement at the wrong hour misses this.
  • Easterday et al., Remote Sensing 11:1853 (2019). Flew a small drone over coyote brush in the Bay Area at different times of day; water stress separated best at low altitude in the early morning.
  • Live fuel moisture sampling procedures, NIFC. Fire agencies clip plants between 11:00 and 16:00. Our midday flight matches that window, so our numbers can be compared with theirs.
  • Brown, Jolly et al., Fire Ecology (2025). Links live fuel moisture to how shrubs manage water through the day and season.
  • 14 CFR 107.31, visual line of sight, which sets our 1 km radius.
